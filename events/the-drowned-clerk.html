Event · 1487 AS

The Drowned Clerk

Date
Late in the winter of 1487 AS, before the thaw
Place
The town ditch of Ocristan, below the east wall
Dead
Iacob Serdan, a clerk of Baron Mihail's chancery
Finding
Self-drowned, by the city's ditch-wardens. Widely disbelieved.
Left
A note: I sold it twice.
Copies of the terms
At least three: the chancery's, Vasselt's, and one more. Who holds the third is unknown.

Iacob Serdan was the clerk who made a copy of the Ustrus loan terms and sold it to Bloria's factor in Ocristan. Late in the winter of 1487 AS he was found in the town ditch below the east wall. Everyone who knew of the copy assumed he had been killed for it, and each suspected someone else. The city's ditch-wardens found that he had drowned himself. He left a note of four words, which showed that the factor's copy was not the only one.

The clerk

Serdan was a Veleh man of about thirty, the son of a notary, who had learned the southern tongue in the Ustrus customs house at the Vorn Danu crossing. Mihail's chancery took him on in 1487 AS for that reason. When Mihail came back from Beit Haifud with the signed loan (see The Gold Run), it was Serdan who made the fair copy kept in the chancery. He was one of perhaps three people in Ocristan who could read it.

Over the winter he made a second copy and sold it to Edrun Vasselt. Vasselt kept it from Bloria and sold his silence to Mihail instead.

The ditch

Serdan was found face down in the ice of the ditch, which is shallow below the east wall. His coat had been weighted. The ditch-wardens, who pull a dozen bodies a winter out of the town ditch, recorded three points:

  • The weights were chancery lead, the kind used to hold paper flat, sewn into the lining with the chancery's waxed thread. The stitching was neat and had taken time.
  • There was no mark of a blow, a cord or a struggle.
  • His room above a saddler's was locked from inside. On the table, under his key, was a scrap of chancery paper in his own hand: I sold it twice.

The wardens recorded him as self-drowned. Their finding has been accepted by nobody with a stake in it.

Suspicions

Vasselt is said to have believed Mihail had the clerk killed, to make Vasselt's copy the only one outside the chancery. Mihail's household is said to have believed it was Vasselt, closing the trail back to himself. Others named the Counting-House, which would have reason to stop a leak of its own terms. Each version required a murder, and the wardens' finding fitted none of them.

The note was taken from the wardens by men of Mihail's household within a day. It is known only because one of the wardens repeated it.

The second sale

If the note is honest, Serdan sold the terms to two buyers, not one. Vasselt was one. The other is unknown. Those suggested include:

  • Wendel Raust, the Ostvald reckoner, who was in Ocristan that winter reading Vasselt's books. If he bought a copy, the Ostvald houses may know what Bloria's crown does not.
  • Whoever in western Veleh reports to Mechil. See The Mechil Embassy.
  • The Counting-House itself, buying back its own terms to learn where the leak was.

Why Serdan killed himself is also unknown. He had been paid, and nobody is known to have threatened him. Those who worked beside him in the chancery say he had been reading the fair copy again in the weeks before he died, alone, after hours. The common reading is that he came to understand what the terms meant, and what he had done by selling them to the second buyer. What the terms say is not known to this atlas.

Loose threads

  • Who bought the second copy, and has it been read yet by anyone who can act on it?
  • What did Serdan understand in the fair copy that he had not understood when he made it?
  • The note was taken by Mihail's household. Does Mihail know the terms have reached a third reader?
