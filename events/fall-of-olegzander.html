Event · 1487 AS

Fall of King Olegzander

Date
1487 AS, from the fall of the Rift to the second council vote and the weeks after
Place
Ocristan
Fallen
King Olegzander, Ustrus's proxy
Cause
A false story that he sold the Rift
Author of the story
Baron Mihail's household, before the siege began
Killed by
Men of the Hundred-Eleven, on Mihail's order

King Olegzander lost his crown in 1487 AS to a story that he had sold the Rift to Baron Mihail. The story was false, and it was Mihail's. It had been written before the first stone was thrown at the Rift's walls, and it was waiting in Ocristan when the fortress fell. A few months later, when the council had been bought, men of the Hundred-Eleven killed Olegzander in a house near the west gate.

The story

Within days of the fall of the Rift, criers, carters and tavern talk in Ocristan all carried the same account. Lord Andrej had been sold from inside. The king had arranged it, for Mihail's money or Ustrus's. The main gate had been unbarred from inside, at the hour the assault began.

The account was complete from the first telling. It named the hour and the gate, and it did not change as it spread, which rumours usually do. Nobody in Ocristan could say who had told it first.

Who wrote it

The story was Mihail's. The case rests on one detail: the gate it named was real. Men of the Hundred-Eleven did help open the gate from inside, near enough that hour, to let Mihail's army in. See Lord Andrej. Only those who had planned the opening knew it would happen, and when. The story put a traitor's name on the company's work.

It was also ready too soon. The Rift held for five weeks, and the story was in Ocristan within days of the end. Men of Mihail's household are known to have gone to the capital before the siege began, carrying letters to taverns and print-shops that had no business with a baron. The text was written in advance and released when the gate was opened. Whether Mihail would have used it if the siege had failed is unknown.

Olegzander was a likely traitor in the eyes of the city. He was hated for the tax press, he was Ustrus's creature, and the rot made people ready to believe anything of him. Ustrus could have contradicted the story and did not. See The Counting-House.

The crown

The council would not keep a king believed to have sold the fortress that guarded Ustrus's tax road. It made Olegzander give up the crown, then refused Mihail's claim too. Olegzander kept his council seat as a voievod. Mihail went south for gold; see The Gold Run.

When the gold came back to Ocristan, the council was bought or killed. Olegzander was not offered money. When the council voted again, his chair was empty.

The house by the west gate

After the vote Olegzander went to ground in a house near the west gate, owned by a dyer who had once been one of his riders. He was there some weeks. Then a squad of the Hundred-Eleven came, on an order that passed from Mihail through Elric. The dyer's household was killed with him. The house was cleaned and later sold. No body was shown, and none was buried under his name.

Like every man the company has killed, he is said to be written in the Grimoire. The binding stops any of the squad from speaking of it, which is why no account of his last hour exists.

He died owing. Olegzander's rot was the interest on a bargain with a witch of the Blackmire, and a Blackmire debt passes on when the buyer dies: to his heirs, and failing them, to the people who lived under him. By killing him Mihail moved the rest of that debt onto someone else. Whether he knew this, and whether he cared, is unknown.

Loose threads

  • Mihail's men carried the story to Ocristan before the siege. Who printed it, and are they still alive?
  • The dyer had been one of Olegzander's tax riders. Who told the company where the king was hiding?
  • Ustrus never denied the story. Did anyone in Beit Haifud know it was coming?
