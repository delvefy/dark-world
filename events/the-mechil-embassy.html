Event · 1488 AS

The Mechil Embassy

Date
Spring 1488 AS, during the rising at Trantar
Place
The guest hall of the Rookery, a mute monastery in the forests of north-eastern Veleh, near the Mechil border
Envoy
Baron Mihail, in person, with a small escort
Received by
Envoys of Mechil; number and rank unknown
Offered
Default on the Ustrus loan; Veleh out of Ustrus's hands
Answer
None

In the spring of 1488 AS, while the Hundred-Eleven fought at Trantar, Baron Mihail rode east in secret to meet envoys of Mechil. It was the one empire around Veleh that was not paying him. He offered to break with Ustrus. The envoys heard him out and gave no answer.

Why Mechil

By 1488 AS Mihail was in debt to two empires. Bloria paid him openly as a proxy against Ustrus. Ustrus had lent him the gold he spent on the council in Ocristan, to be repaid in something other than gold. See The Gold Run. Mechil had not moved on Veleh in about a hundred years. Of the three powers behind the empires, its one was the only one with no claim on him.

Mihail's problem was the Ustrus creditors. When they came to Ocristan to collect, he would have to pay whatever he had signed for, or refuse and face the empire that had held Veleh for fifty years. Bloria's money would not stop an Ustrus army. Mechil, perhaps, could.

The offer

What Mihail proposed is known in outline from people around him:

  • He would default on the Ustrus loan and pay nothing of what he had signed for.
  • He would close the tax road and send the Counting-House's riders back across the Vorn Danu.
  • Veleh would be out of Ustrus's hands.
  • In return, Mechil would stand behind him when the creditors came, whether with a word, an army on the border, or both.

Whether he offered Mechil anything more for itself is unknown.

The answer

The envoys came. They listened to Mihail for two days. They asked questions, few and exact. They made no promise and no refusal, and left the way they had come. No word has followed from Mechil since. The empire has answered Veleh this way, by not answering, for a century.

One detail is told by men of Mihail's escort. On the second day the envoys asked how many of the Hundred-Eleven were still alive. Mihail did not yet know that Trantar had fallen. The envoys did.

Trantar

The rising at Trantar drew an Ustrus army and its mages into the far west at the same time as Mihail was in the north-east. Whether this was planned is disputed. One reading has the rising raised to pull Ustrus's eyes west, and not much minded whether it won. The other has two separate plans that happened to fall in the same season. The company paid either way: it came back from Trantar with 39 men.

Whether Elric knew where his employer was, or why, is unknown. If the company knew, the binding of the Grimoire would stop any of its men from saying so.

Loose threads

  • The envoys knew Trantar had fallen before Mihail did. Who in the west reports to Mechil? Narrowed: the belief in the north-east is that Mechil reads crows, and listens at the Rookery. Unconfirmed.
  • Bloria pays Mihail to oppose Ustrus, not to court Mechil. Does Bloria know he went east? Narrowed: Bloria's factor in Ocristan, Edrun Vasselt, knew he was gone. Whether he knew where, or told Bloria, is unknown.
  • Mechil gave no answer. When the Ustrus creditors come to Ocristan, will Mihail act as if it did?
