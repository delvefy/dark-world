The Gold Run

Date
1487 AS, about ten weeks
Route
Ocristan, the Vorn Danu crossing, Beit Haifud; north through Ustrus to the coast; by sea to Strova; overland to Ocristan
Principal
Baron Mihail
Escort
The Hundred-Eleven under Elric; Mihail's cavalry guard
Cargo
Ustrus gold, about a hundred bars of some twelve kilograms each
Opposed by
Bloodhounds and two unflagged galleys. Sent from inside Ustrus; by whom is disputed
Losses
About forty of Mihail's cavalry; at least eight of the company

The Gold Run is the name given in Veleh to Baron Mihail's journey to Beit Haifud in 1487 AS and the return with Ustrus gold. The Emperor of Ustrus gave the gold. Someone else in Ustrus spent a good deal of money trying to make sure it never arrived.

Background

After the fall of the Rift, the council in Ocristan forced Olegzander off the throne and then refused Mihail's claim too. Mihail could not buy the council with Blorian money alone. He went south to ask the overlord whose proxy he had just helped bring down. Captured mercenaries die slowly in Ustrus, so the company went in under orders of secrecy.

South

The column crossed the Vorn Danu on the builders' bridge. Two days onto the Ustrus side the bloodhounds found it.

The hounds were red-furred, with yellow eye-shine and teeth as long as a finger. Their howls sounded nearly human. They hunted in packs of ten, in waves, and their bites smoked from the inside and went on steaming through the dressings. They were not wild dogs. The waves came at intervals that implied handlers somewhere behind the line.

The run lasted about ten days. Mihail's cavalry guard took most of the losses, perhaps forty men. The company lost four: three to the dogs, and one boy who twisted an ankle and was left behind with a dagger and a water skin. The hunt ended when the company ambushed the handlers in a creek bed. None of the handlers is recorded as taken alive.

Beit Haifud

The young Emperor of Ustrus, Tamarun, received Mihail in his throne room. The audience was held in the southern tongue. Mihail knelt without bowing his back. Elric stood among the escort and followed the talk. Whether he took part in it is not recorded.

The Emperor opened a chest of gold. Afterward, in a windowless courtyard, Elric had each man of the company strap a bar under his clothes. The chest was refilled with dirt and sent off as a decoy. The plan relied on the binding of the Grimoire: a bound man cannot desert, cannot betray the company and cannot sell what he carries.

The loan

The men who carried the gold believed it was a gift. It was not. Mihail put his name to a document in the southern tongue before he left the palace. Its terms have never been published. What is known of them comes at second hand, from people around the Baron:

  • It was a loan.
  • The repayment is not in gold. What it is (a service, a place, a person) is unknown. Widely suspected: war on Bloria. See Tamarun.
  • The creditors will come to Ocristan to collect.

Elric would have understood the document. If the company's binding prevents its men from revealing the contract's secrets, it may also cover this.

North and by sea

The company left by the west gate at midnight and turned north off the road at once. The day after the audience, the garrisons on the southern road were doubled. Mihail rode east with twenty cavalrymen and an empty supply train to draw the pursuit, and bought the company two days.

The company crossed northern Ustrus on foot through scrub and dry creek beds and reached a small port on the Eastern Sea. Two broad coasters were waiting, with a false floor already built into the hold. Someone had arranged that weeks in advance. The bars went under the planks, and the coasters sailed north along the coast.

On the sixth day at sea two galleys without flags came out of the east at dawn and closed with boarding-hooks. The fight was hand to hand on decks fifteen feet across. Mihail came up from the hold without a helmet. The sea around him went flat in a ring while the water beyond it kept rolling. The company lost four men. Both galleys were burned, and their dead went over the side without being searched.

The coasters landed at a fishing town in Strova. The gold went inland to Ocristan in two iron-bound boxes on a different wagon, while the company marched as a decoy. It reached the capital about ten days later, and was spent there. See Ocristan.

Who sent the hunters

Bloria was paying Mihail, and had no reason to stop him. The weight of evidence points back into Ustrus.

  • Hounds of that breed are not known to be kept in Veleh or Bloria. Southern travellers describe red hounds in the kennels of Ustrus border lords.
  • The galleys came out of the open sea and showed no flags, but they were of southern build.
  • The hunt began within two days of the crossing. Whoever sent it knew the route before the column set out.
  • Someone doubled the southern garrisons the day after the Emperor had paid.

So one hand in Ustrus paid Mihail and another tried to kill him. Two readings are current:

  • The counting-house. The office in Beit Haifud that ran Veleh's taxes through Olegzander lost its man, and with him its press. It did not accept the Emperor's new arrangement.
  • The hand behind the throne. Whichever of the Thirteen stands behind Ustrus acted over the Emperor's head, or against him. If so, the Emperor and his patron want different things in Veleh.

The two readings are not exclusive, and may be one: the Counting-House is widely held to answer to Ustrus's patron, not to the Emperor. Neither reading has been confirmed.

Loose threads

  • What did Mihail sign? If the price is a person, whose name is in the document?
  • Someone had the coasters and the false floor ready weeks before the audience. Who in Ustrus knew the Emperor would pay before he did?
  • The galley dead went over the side unsearched. Did any of them wash up on the Strova shore?
  • The sea went still around Mihail, as a stretch of the Vorn Danu is said to go still one night a year. Is it the same stillness?
