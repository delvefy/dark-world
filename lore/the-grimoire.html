The Grimoire

Kind
Bound relic with a will of its own
Binds
The Hundred-Eleven: 111 men, exactly
Keeper
Elric, Centurion
Made by
Unknown. Not the Thirteen.
Refuses
Mages (lethally); women (company policy)

The Grimoire is a tome bound in human skin and stitched with thick black thread. It is warm to the touch, like a feverish hand. It binds the mercenary company called the Hundred-Eleven, and it decides what that company is.

The book

Its pages are yellow and carry two columns of names. Every soul the company has ever taken is written there, tens of thousands in all. The book looks no thicker than a few hundred pages, and it never runs out of space. It is kept in a locked chest. Only the Centurion decides when it is opened and who receives its healing draught.

The Grimoire has its own will. It was not made by the Thirteen, and none of them has taken it, though they want it. Who made it, and when, is unknown.

The binding

A recruit puts his hand on the book and swears allegiance to the Grimoire. It feels like every bone breaking at once. Purple light runs up the arm and across the chest, and the recruit loses consciousness for two or three days. When he wakes, both hands carry the number 111. In 1487 AS one such recruit was a street boy from Tullanon.

The company always has 111 men: a Centurion (Number One), ten Deans (Two to Eleven) and a hundred soldiers in squads of ten. When a man dies, every higher number moves down one, and the tattoos change on the skin.

What it gives

  • Fast healing. Burns, deep cuts and arrow wounds close in days.
  • Strength and endurance. Veterans carry double loads, march for days, and can outrun horses over short distances.
  • Partial, unreliable resistance to magic.

All of it grows with rank. New soldiers feel it lightly. Deans are close to unkillable.

What it takes

  • It must be fed. The book makes its men kill: a named target, any civilian, or one a day. Resisting only increases the pressure until the body acts on its own.
  • A bound man cannot harm a superior or a fellow of the company.
  • He cannot betray the company's secrets, even under torture.
  • He cannot desert. The book finds you.
  • A mage who swears the oath dies on the spot; the skin melts like wax.

Employers hire the Hundred-Eleven less for their skill than for this: a bound company cannot betray its contract. That is why its men carried Ustrus gold out of Beit Haifud in 1487 AS strapped under their shirts.

Loose threads

  • What happens to the names in the book? Are they a record, or a larder?
  • If the Grimoire refuses mages, what would it do with one of the Thirteen?
  • There are said to be other bound relics with other companies. Has the Hundred-Eleven ever met one? Answered in part: three others are known (the Drum, the Chain, the Grey Standard), and by the custom called the Courtesy no two bound companies have met in the field. Whether the Grimoire ever met one before that is unknown. See Bound Relics.
