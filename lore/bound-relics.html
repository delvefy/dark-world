Four, counting the Grimoire; others rumoured

A will, a binding, a gift and a price. Little else.

All of the Thirteen, it is said

A bound relic is an object that binds a company of men to itself. Each has a will of its own, gives its men something no training could, and takes something from them in return. The Grimoire of the Hundred-Eleven is the best known in Veleh. Three others are known well enough to describe, and more are rumoured. They are few, and no two of them work alike: each binds by a different count, gives a different gift, and takes a different price. In the whole of the record no two bound companies have met in battle.

The Grimoire

The Grimoire binds 111 men, exactly. It gives healing, strength and some resistance to magic, more with rank. It takes killing: its men must feed it, and every soul they take is written in its pages. It kills any mage who swears to it. Its keeper in 1488 AS is Elric. See The Grimoire.

The Drum

A war-drum of black hide on a frame of dark wood, carried in the far south by a company of foot called the Hollow March. It binds as many men as can hear it beaten on the night of their oath. The company has numbered between three hundred and seven hundred in different accounts.

  • Gift. While the Drum beats, its men do not tire, do not sleep and do not feel wounds until the beating stops. The Hollow March is said to have marched four days without a halt and fought at the end of it.
  • Price. Their hearing. A man who swears to the Drum hears it, and over the following year hears less and less of anything else. Veterans of the March hear nothing at all but the Drum, wherever it is, and can find it across a country by the sound.

The March has fought in the southern wars of Ustrus, for pay. It has never come north of the Vorn Danu.

The Chain

A ship's chain of black iron, about sixty links long, kept coiled in the hold of a galley of the Eastern Sea. Each link has a man. The company is called the Links, and it hires out as marines and boarders to whoever will pay, mostly in the southern trade.

  • Gift. No man of the Links can drown. They go overboard in armour and walk back up the hull. They fight on wet decks as if on a parade ground.
  • Price. None of them can go further from the Chain than a man can shout. A Link who goes ashore and walks inland is found dead on the road at that distance, and his link is warm when they open the hold. They live, marry and die on the galley.

The Links are not known to have been aboard either of the unflagged galleys that attacked the Ustrus gold in 1487 AS. See The Gold Run. Men of the Hundred-Eleven who fought on that day say they would have known.

The Grey Standard

A cavalry banner of grey cloth carried in the old border wars of the north, between the empires there. It binds every man who has once ridden under it into battle. There is no fixed number.

  • Gift. Its riders do not break. No charge of the Grey Standard is recorded as having turned back, and no line it struck held.
  • Price. They cannot retreat while it stands, and they cannot be buried. When a rider dies his hair is cut and woven into the banner. The banner is now very large and very heavy, and the company that carries it is small. It is said to need four men to lift.

Where the Grey Standard is in 1488 AS is unknown. It has not been reported in the field for some years.

Rumoured

Mercenaries and soldiers tell of others: a bell that binds a garrison to its tower, a mask that binds whoever wears it to the last man who did, a set of dice. None has been traced to a company anyone can name. A cautious reading is that the four above are all that exist in the lands around Veleh, and that the rest are what soldiers say on long nights.

One proposal goes further. It holds that the ledger of the Counting-House in Beit Haifud, whose orders appear unsigned in its margin, is a bound relic that binds a house of clerks rather than a company of soldiers. See The Margin. Nothing proves it.

Kept apart

Bound companies are hired by rival powers and sometimes fight in the same wars. They have never fought each other. Mercenary custom calls this the Courtesy: a bound company does not take a contract in a war where another bound company is already engaged, and an employer who has one does not hire a second. Nobody knows who made the rule. Everyone who hires bound companies keeps it.

Some think the relics keep it themselves. Soldiers of the Hundred-Eleven say that Elric has turned down contracts without giving a reason, and that the book was open on the table when he did. Whether the Grimoire has ever met another relic, in this century or before it, is unknown. If it has, the company could not say.

Origins

Where the relics come from is unknown. The Grimoire was not made by the Thirteen. Whether the others were is not known. Elric crossed the Eastern Sea from the uncharted lands around 1424 AS, and some think the Grimoire, and perhaps all of them, came from there. Others point out that the Drum and the Chain have been in the south as long as the south has records. All of the Thirteen are said to want bound relics. None is known to hold one.

Loose threads

  • Who made the Courtesy, and what happens to the company that breaks it?
  • A veteran of the Hollow March can hear the Drum across a country. Can a man of the Hundred-Eleven feel the Grimoire the same way, and from how far?
  • The Grey Standard has not been seen in the field for years. Is it lost, or is it waiting for riders?
