The Counting-House is the name used in Veleh for the office in Beit Haifud that taxes Ustrus's subject lands. It ran Veleh's revenue for about fifty years through placed men, the last and worst of them Olegzander. It keeps two ledgers, one for goods and one for people. It answers to whichever of the Thirteen stands behind Ustrus, and not to the Emperor.

The first ledger

The goods ledger is the one Veleh knows. It was carried by riders in town livery with leather boxes, twice a year, to every village the house could find. The rate was half of what a household could spare: half the grain, half the cattle, half the worked iron, at a fixed and low price. Refusers were beaten. Town levies, paid for by merchant houses such as Karavin, stood behind the riders. The house set the rate and the proxy took the blame.

The second ledger

The house also taxes in lives. When a village cannot pay in goods, or has rebelled, it pays in people. The second ledger records who is owed:

  • The corps. Children who show a talent for magic are taken south to the Ustrus mage corps. They are not seen again as themselves.
  • The oars. Grown men go to the galleys, chained to the bench.
  • The estates. Everyone else goes to the southern plantations and quarries, where the heat kills northerners quickly.

For this the house needs names. A burned village is worth more to it than a standing one, because its survivors scatter to kin in other valleys, and only the register says who they are. After the rising at Trantar the riders took the church register of Haldevin before they burned the church. It is presumed to be in the second ledger now.

In Veleh the people-levy has been rare enough to be half-believed. The house has kept it quiet by letting proxies take the blame for whoever disappeared.

Its master

Officially the Counting-House serves the Emperor. Its conduct in 1487 AS makes little sense on that reading.

  • The young Emperor lent Baron Mihail gold, knowing it would unseat the house's own man in Ocristan.
  • The house had hunted Mihail with bloodhounds on his way south, before the audience. After the audience it doubled the garrisons on the southern road and sent galleys after the gold. See The Gold Run.
  • It has outlasted every emperor in living memory, and its orders do not pass through the palace.

The view held in Veleh, and quietly in parts of Ustrus, is that the house belongs to the power behind the throne: the one of the Thirteen who stands behind Ustrus. The Emperor went around it in 1487 AS. Whether he did so knowing what he was going around is unconfirmed. See Tamarun. Either way, Ustrus has two wills in Veleh, and the Counting-House is the older one.

The Rift

The Rift guarded the tax road. When Mihail besieged it, the house sent no relief in five weeks, though Lord Andrej was no enemy of Ustrus. Why is not known. A fortress that pays no tax and cannot be taken may have been worth less to the house than a fallen one with a debt attached.

Loose threads

  • What does Ustrus's patron want with people that it takes them by name?
  • Mihail's loan is to be repaid in something other than gold. Is it on the second ledger?
  • Who heads the Counting-House? No Veleh record names a face.
