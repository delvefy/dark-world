Lore · Material

Kimberstone

Colour
White; does not weather or stain
Worked by
The builders, before the Silence. No one since.
Harmed by
No tool, fire or engine. Only certain magics, at great cost.
Known works
The Rift; the Vorn Danu bridge legs; the pillars under old Tullanon; scattered ruins
Known breaks
The Rift's seventh tower; the Vorn Danu bridge

Kimberstone is the white stone of the builders who vanished at the Silence. It cannot be cut, chipped, burned or broken by any mechanical means. A few magics can damage it, and those that have are known to have cost the caster everything. No one living knows how it was made or shaped.

Properties

Kimberstone is white and stays white. It does not weather, take moss or hold a stain; rain runs off it and blood washes off it. The builders' works have no joints or mortar. The Rift is said to be cut from a single mass, and nobody has found a seam to prove otherwise.

Tools do nothing to it. Picks blunt, saws dull, and drills turn without biting. Siege engines are no better. At the Rift in 1487 AS Mihail's trebuchets threw stone at the walls for five weeks. They smashed the timber hoardings and the ordinary masonry later lords had added, and broke the gate. The kimberstone underneath them was left without a mark. This is why Mihail needed men inside: the walls could not be breached, only the doors. See Fall of the Rift.

Ordinary mage-fire does no better. Lord Andrej's half-sister burned men on the Rift's own walls, and the walls did not scorch.

What breaks it

Kimberstone is known to have been broken only twice, and both cases took magic of a rare kind.

  • The seventh tower of the Rift. The wizard Aran burned his own soul to break it, centuries ago. The tower snapped in half, and Aran did not survive. The upper half fell into the Krisen and lies there still.
  • The Vorn Danu bridge. Broken in a war nobody remembers, leaving only the legs in the current. Who broke it, and what it cost them, is unknown. A span that size was not broken cheaply.

Which magics can harm kimberstone, and why, is disputed. The one well-attested case cost the caster his soul. Many hold that this is the rule, not the exception: the stone yields only to magic paid for with the caster's own life. Others think there are other ways, and that the empires' mage corps would very much like to find one. Whether any of the Thirteen can break it is unknown.

The pillars of Tullanon

The oldest quarter of Tullanon stands on pillars that have never rotted. They are kimberstone, sheathed in timber so that they look like the rest. The people of the quarter replace the timber casing as it rots and say nothing about what is inside. That means the builders' works reached the western swamps, and the town grew up on a builders' ruin without most of its people knowing.

Why the quarter hides it is not recorded. A fortress made of kimberstone cannot be taken from its lord. A pillar can't be taken either, but the ground around it can.

Theories

  • A lid. The old belief that the builders went down rather than away, and that kimberstone covers whatever they went down into. Nothing has ever been seen under it, because nobody can dig through it.
  • A signature. Kimberstone is simply what the builders built with, the way later peoples build in brick. It lasts because they knew something that was lost at the Silence.
  • A relic. Kimberstone is not a material but a working, a magic still running. If so, something still powers it.

None of these is proven, and each is held with more confidence than the evidence allows.
