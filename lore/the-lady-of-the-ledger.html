The Lady of the Ledger is what Blorians call, quietly, the power behind their crown. Kings of Bloria live and die like other men. At the side of each of them, at every coronation in the rolls, stands a woman of middle years in some high household office: chancellor, confessor, keeper of the seals, dowager. Her name and title change with each reign. The court records describe her face the same way every time. Everyone at court knows this, and nobody says it.

The coronations

A Blorian king is crowned in two parts. The first is public: the oath, the anointing, the crown. The second takes place in a closed room afterward. The new king signs the crown's book of debts, accepting every obligation his predecessor left. The book is held open for him by an officer of the household, whose title is different in every reign.

The coronation rolls name that officer. Over some centuries they name a chancellor, two confessors, a dowager queen with no surviving children, a keeper of the seals, and a mistress of the privy roll. Each is described as a woman of middle years, pale-eyed, with a low voice. Court painters have left portraits of several of them. The portraits are kept in different houses and are not hung together. Those who have seen more than one say they show one woman.

The name Lady of the Ledger comes from the closed room. She holds the book; the king signs it.

At court

The Lady's offices are always household offices. She has never sat on a council, led an army or held land in her own name. She does not appear to give orders. Courtiers who have served near her describe her as courteous, very well informed, and never surprised.

Her tenure ends quietly. Late in each reign the officer retires to the country, or dies, and is buried without ceremony. Early in the next reign a new officer of the same description takes up a household post. Nobody at the Blorian court has been known to remark on it in writing. Two who did so aloud, within the last century, left court soon afterward. One of them went into bond.

The ledger and the bond

The crown owes the Ostvald houses more than it could pay in a century. The houses take people when their debts go unpaid, hold them in the Bonds, and after seven years send them north to Kalderhof. What is done to the debtors there is unknown. Blorians assume the Lady is its end: that the debts, the Bonds and the lower bond are one engine, and that the years taken at Kalderhof are what keep her at middle age.

Nothing written links the Lady to Kalderhof. The connection is a belief, widely held, and unconfirmed.

In 1488 AS

The household officer most Blorians take for the Lady in 1488 AS is Lady Hedwen, Mistress of the Privy Roll. She held the book at the coronation of the present king, Eberwin III, in 1461 AS, and courtiers in Aldrecht say she has not changed since. She is not known to have expressed a view on Veleh.

Bloria funds Baron Mihail against Ustrus, through its factor Edrun Vasselt. The houses sent their own reckoner, Wendel Raust, to audit him, and tried to buy the Hundred-Eleven over Mihail's head. If the Lady sets the houses' policy, then she is not satisfied to pay Mihail. She wants to own what he owes.

What is not known

  • What she is, and where the Thirteen came from. See The Thirteen.
  • Whether the officer at each coronation is one woman, or a line of women trained to be taken for one.
  • Whether she has ever left Bloria.
  • What she wants with the Grimoire, if she wants it. All the Thirteen are said to.

Loose threads

  • Two courtiers spoke of her aloud. One went into bond. Who was the other, and where did he go? Answered in part: Kaspar Hollmar, head of a failed Ostvald house, c. 1458 AS; he fled the night he spoke, by report to the mute brothers of the Rookery in Veleh (see Kaspar Hollmar). See The Hollmar Failure.
  • The book the king signs holds every crown debt. Does it also hold the names of the bonded, and is Kalderhof's count in it? Answered in part: every bonded name is entered there, and a seal holds only while its name stands. See Blood-Seals. Whether Kalderhof is counted is unknown.
