The Last Dawn is the figure, or the event, that a single old fragment says will return and destroy the Thirteen. It is the only prophecy about the Thirteen that every realm knows. No realm admits to believing it.

The fragment

The prophecy comes from a few lines near the start of the oldest annals, among the first entries written after the blank that the calendar calls the Silence. The original is not known to survive. Every version in circulation is a copy of a copy, and the copies disagree on the words that matter most. A common rendering in Veleh runs:

Thirteen there are, and thirteen they stay, through the long night, until the Last Dawn returns; and after it there will be none.

The disputes:

  • Returns, or is returned. Some copies have the Last Dawn coming back of its own accord. Others have it being brought back, which would mean someone brings it.
  • A name, or a time. Some copies read the Last Dawn as a title, someone who returns. Others read at the last dawn, a moment and not a person.
  • None of what. After it there will be none may mean no more Thirteen. It may also mean no more dawns. This is the root of the two readings Veleh still argues over: promise or warning.

The phrase through the long night is in every copy. Most readers take it to mean the wait is long: generations, not years.

The candle

In Veleh, on the worst night of the long night, some households leave a candle burning in an east-facing window. Nobody says it is for the Last Dawn. Everyone knows it is. Tax riders have been known to put the candles out, and in some villages to fine the household. That is the nearest thing to an official position any power has taken.

Who looks for it

Nobody, openly. All three empires treat the prophecy as peasant superstition and say so. No mage corps admits to searching for it, and no court has ever named a candidate in order to hunt one. If the Thirteen fear the fragment, they have left no sign of it, except that their servants go out of their way to put out candles in the snow.

False dawns

Every generation in Veleh names someone. A rebel who survived a hanging. A child born on the worst night of the long night. A stranger from over the sea. None has ended anything. Most were killed for some other reason (rebellion, debt, theft), which the empires take care to point out.

In 1488 AS the talk in Veleh's inns turns now and then to Baron Mihail, because he came back from the dead and the prophecy's word is return. It is not serious talk. Mihail takes money from two of the empires behind the Thirteen. The Last Dawn, whatever it is, has not come.

Loose threads

  • Where are the oldest annals kept, and has anyone alive seen the page itself?
