Ilinca is the daughter of Lord Andrej, the last lord of the Rift, and the elder of his two surviving children. When the Rift fell in 1487 AS, Baron Mihail left her father to die in a cage and took her and her brother Vasko into his own household. They have lived there since, as his wards. The Rift is now held in the boy's name. The girl is said to have learned something from her nurse, who was a mind-witch.

The fall

When the gate was opened in 1487 AS, Andrej's children were in the citadel with the women of the household. Mihail's men found them in a cellar under the chapel on the second day of the street fighting. By the account of one of the men who found them, the girl had her hand over her brother's mouth and had told him, quite calmly, that the soldiers could not see them. Two of the soldiers walked past the cellar door before a third looked in.

Their father was taken at the breach and put in a cage in the square below the citadel, in the sun, without water. The children were kept in the citadel above it for the days it took him to die. Whether they were made to watch, or were only able to, is not recorded. See Fall of the Rift.

Mihail did not visit Andrej in the cage. He did go to the children. He is recorded as standing in the doorway of their room for some time and leaving without speaking. The next morning he gave orders that they were to be fed, dressed according to their rank, and taken with his household when it left.

The wards

Since 1487 AS the two children have lived in Mihail's household, in Ocristan when he is there and with his baggage when he is not. They are taught letters, riding and the law of inheritance, by tutors Mihail pays. They are not kept locked up and have never tried to run. They are always attended.

The arrangement is legal. Under Veleh custom a lord's minor heir may be put under the wardship of a peer, and a ward's lands are held in the ward's name until he comes of age. Vasko is Lord of the Rift. The fortress is held for him by a castellan of Mihail's choosing, and its revenues go to his guardian. Andrej's surviving soldiers serve in the garrison under that arrangement. They serve the boy. Mihail's men in the garrison hold the gate.

When what was left of the Hundred-Eleven came back to the Rift after the defeat at Trantar in 1488 AS, the garrison let them in without asking who they were. It was holding the fortress for a child in the keeping of the man the company served.

The girl

Ilinca's nurse was the mind-witch of Andrej's household, the woman whose spells the company remembered as ghostly figures and cold fingers inside the skull. Andrej's house was known for raising its mages at home from childhood. The nurse had the girl from infancy. Whether she was teaching her, and how far she had got, nobody now living can say.

What Mihail's household reports is little, and none of it is tested:

  • Servants say that when the girl is angry they feel a cold pressure behind the eyes, and that it stops when she leaves the room.
  • Dogs will not stay in her chamber.
  • The cellar story: two soldiers looked straight at the door and did not see it.
  • Mihail's rooms are cold. Everyone who goes into them says so. Ilinca is the only person in the household who has been heard to say they are not.

She speaks to her tutors and to her brother. She has not been heard to speak to Mihail. He addresses her by her full style, as the daughter of a lord, and has never been heard to raise his voice to her.

Why he keeps them

Mihail has not said. Three explanations are current in Ocristan:

  • The Rift. The simplest. A fortress held in its own heir's name keeps its old garrison loyal and gives the council nothing to object to. A dead heir would have left the Rift as plain conquest.
  • The gift. Veleh has no mage corps, and the empires do not lend their mages. Andrej's house was the only one in Veleh that knew how to raise its own. If the girl has her nurse's gift, Mihail has the only such mage in the kingdom, and she is young enough to be raised to his use.
  • The debt. Mihail's family bought his return from a witch of the Blackmire with his three youngest children. Blackmire debts that are not paid in full fall on the buyer's heirs. Mihail has no children left that anyone knows of. Some in Ocristan say he is not raising wards but heirs, and that he means to have someone to leave the rest of the bill to. This is said quietly, and it is unconfirmed.

The explanations are not exclusive.

Loose threads

  • Who is Mihail's castellan at the Rift, and does he answer to the baron or to the boy? Answered in part: Grigore Tal, a man of the Crow's Pass garrison, who holds Andrej's old soldiers to their oath to the boy. See Grigore Tal.
  • The nurse had the girl from infancy. Did she leave anything written, anywhere, about how the house raised its mages?
  • If Mihail names the children his heirs, does the Blackmire's claim on him pass to them?
