Olegzander was Ustrus's man in Ocristan. He ran the tax press as a voievod and then wore Veleh's crown for two years. He fell in 1487 AS when he was blamed for a betrayal at the Rift that never took place, and he is almost certainly dead.

The tax press

For years before his crown, Olegzander collected Ustrus's taxes in Veleh. His riders came to the villages twice a year with his papers and took half of everything a household could spare: half the cattle, half the grain, half the worked iron. The price was fixed and low, and those who refused were beaten. The merchant house of Karavin funded the town levies that backed the riders.

The press did not stop when he became king; it got worse. The hatred his riders left in the west fed the rising at Trantar in 1488 AS, a year after he was gone.

The crown

The last king was killed in 1484 AS, and a dozen barons raised levies. In 1485 AS the council of barons ended the contest the way Ustrus wanted. It crowned Olegzander, whom no baron could beat while Ustrus stood behind him. Barons who had raised levies were paid to send them home. Baron Mihail took the money and kept his men.

The rot

The name the Putrid was not only an insult. His flesh was rotting. Those who stood near him at court describe a sweet, wet smell under heavy scent, gloves he never took off, and a collar worn high in summer. The rot never killed him and never seemed to get worse.

It was the mark of a bargain with a witch of the Blackmire, the same tradition said to have raised Mihail at Crow's Pass. Blackmire witches sell years and take payment in other lives. The flesh of the buyer pays interest. In the villages under his tax press, people claimed that children sickened in the years his riders came hardest. Which witch made the bargain, and whether the debt died with him, is unknown.

The Rift, and the lie

After the fall of the Rift in 1487 AS, a story spread through Ocristan that Lord Andrej had been sold from inside and that the king had done it. It arrived complete, with the hour and the gate named, and nobody could say who first told it.

There was no traitor. The Hundred-Eleven took the Rift by themselves: through the sewer tunnel, the killings in the streets, the bodies in the wells, and the breach. Nobody inside opened anything. The betrayal story was invented, and it worked. The fortress that guarded Ustrus's tax road had fallen, and the council would not keep a king who was believed to have sold it. See The Rift.

Fall and death

The council made Olegzander give up the crown. It then refused Mihail's claim as well, and the throne was empty again. Mihail went to Beit Haifud and came back with Ustrus gold, and the council was bought or killed. The house of Karavin was among the dead. When the council voted again, Olegzander, who had kept his seat as a voievod, did not come to his chair.

By most accounts he died within weeks, in a house near the west gate of Ocristan. There was no funeral, no proclamation and no body on display. If the Baron's company did the killing, his name may be among those the Grimoire keeps. Nobody outside the company can check.

Loose threads

  • Who wrote the lie about the Rift? Mihail gained the most from it. Could the story have been ready before the siege began?
  • A Blackmire witch kept him alive. When the buyer dies, does she collect the rest from his heirs, or from his villages?
  • Ustrus let its own king fall on a lie it could have disproved, then paid his rival. Had it written him off before the Rift fell?
