Wendel Raust is a reckoner of the Ostvald houses, sent to Ocristan in the winter of 1487 AS to audit the paper that flows through Bloria's factor there. He is the most likely answer to the question of whether Bloria keeps a second eye in the city. He was sent by the bankers, not the crown, and he is watching the factor, not the baron. In the same winter he met Elric at the company's barracks and offered the Hundred-Eleven a contract. Elric refused it.

Reckoners

The Ostvald houses do not trust the people who spend their bills. Once every few years each house sends a reckoner to any agent who handles large sums abroad. The reckoner reads the agent's books, matches every cashed bill against the debtor's name on its back, and reports to the house alone. Reckoners travel in undyed grey wool, which is the colour of the houses' paper, and are known by it in every Blorian town. They carry no arms and are rarely harmed. A dead reckoner is followed by another, and the second one brings soldiers.

In Ocristan

Raust arrived in Ocristan after the council had been bought and the killings were over. He took rooms at an inn near the cloth market, within sight of Vasselt's counting-house, and spent most of the winter reading. Vasselt opened his books to him, as he was bound to.

What the books showed is unknown. Vasselt has passed about a tenth of every Blorian bill back to himself, through payees of Baron Mihail's choosing, in return for keeping the Ustrus loan terms from Bloria. A reckoner matching names to bills would be well placed to notice that the same payees recur. Whether Raust noticed, and whether he has reported it, is not known. Vasselt still held his office at the latest report.

The barracks

Late in the winter Raust rode to the barracks of the Hundred-Eleven and asked for the centurion by name. He and Elric spoke alone for less than an hour. Raust then left the city by the west gate, alone, and did not stop at his inn.

The substance of the meeting became known through the company. Raust offered the Hundred-Eleven a contract with the Ostvald houses, paid directly in their bills, to run alongside its contract with Mihail. The company would keep serving Mihail. It would also answer to the houses, and report to them on him. The sums offered were said to be larger than anything Mihail paid.

Elric refused. He gave no reason that anyone repeated. Several explanations are told:

  • The company holds one contract at a time, and Elric is loyal to the contract he has signed. This is consistent with his record.
  • Ostvald bills can be cashed only by their payee, and each carries a debtor's blood. The Grimoire is said to take badly to other people's bindings. This is unconfirmed.
  • Elric already knew, from Mihail or from Vasselt, what happens to Ostvald debtors whose bills are held too long. See Kalderhof. This is speculation.

Whether Elric told Mihail of the offer is unknown.

Significance

If the houses tried to buy the company, they were not content to fund Mihail through the crown's factor. They wanted their own hold on him, and on the men who do his killing. That fits the reading that the houses, not the crown, decide Bloria's policy in Veleh. Behind whom they stand is unknown; see The Thirteen.

Loose threads

  • Raust left by the west gate, not the north road home. Where was he going?
  • If Raust has found the recurring payees, what does a house do to a factor who skims, and to the debtors on the skimmed bills?
  • The houses wanted a report on Mihail. With Elric refusing, who did they ask next?
