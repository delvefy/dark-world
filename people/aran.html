Person · Mage

Aran

Known for
Breaking the seventh tower of the Rift
Date
c. 1100 AS; disputed by a century either way, but before the capture of c. 1187
Sent by
An empire. Which one is not remembered.
Price
His soul, burned in the working
Result
The tower broke; the fortress did not fall; the army never came

Aran was a mage sent to break the Rift open for an army. He burned his own soul to do it and broke the seventh tower in half, which is the best-attested breaking of kimberstone since the Silence. It achieved nothing. The army he was opening the way for never arrived, and nobody now agrees on who sent him.

The working

Aran came to the Rift alone, or with an escort too small to be remembered. He worked from the south bank of the Krisen, facing the seventh tower across the water. Accounts agree on three things: it took a night, the light could be seen from the Olta country, and at the end the tower snapped about halfway up. The upper half fell outward into the river.

It is generally accepted that magic can harm kimberstone only when paid for with the caster's own life, and Aran is the case the idea rests on. Nothing of him was recovered, or nothing that any surviving account was willing to describe.

The army that never came

A broken tower was meant to be a breach. It was not one. The tower's lower half still stands as solid as the wall it is set in, and the break opened nothing but sky. Even had it opened a gap, the gap faced the Krisen, and an army would have needed boats under the walls.

No army came anyway, with boats or without. The garrison waited on the walls for a week and saw no one. No one is known to have tried magic on the walls again. When the Rift was taken in c. 1187 AS, it was taken some other way, and that capture is the last anyone remembers before 1487. What happened to the force Aran was sent ahead of is unknown. It may have been recalled, beaten somewhere else, or never sent at all.

Why the seventh tower is disputed in two ways:

  • The wrong tower. Aran meant to bring down the gate-tower and missed, or misjudged which tower held the gate.
  • The right tower. He was sent to break exactly that tower, by someone who wanted the Rift damaged, not taken: a demonstration that it could be done, at the price of one mage.

Who sent him

Each of the three empires' chronicles, where they mention him at all, says he came from one of the others. Veleh's own records say only a mage from abroad. Mage corps of that age took talented children young and kept them, so Aran was probably trained by whoever sent him. That fits any of the three. It is also possible that whoever sent him answered to one of the Thirteen, but nothing points to which.

The stone in the Krisen

The upper half of the seventh tower lies in the bed of the Krisen below the Rift's south wall. In a dry summer, when the river drops, it shows white under the water. No one has raised it, and no one can cut a piece off it. Fishermen keep their nets away from it. The current runs strangely there, and the river has scoured a deep hole on its downstream side. It is the only piece of kimberstone known to lie loose.

Loose threads

  • Three empires each say another sent him. Which of them is lying, or are all three?
  • Was the army ever sent? If it was, where did it go?
  • The stone in the Krisen is the only loose kimberstone known. Who would want it, if anyone found a way to move it?
