Edrun Vasselt is the Blorian factor in Ocristan, the man through whose hands Bloria's money reaches Baron Mihail. By the winter of 1487 AS he had read the terms of the loan Mihail signed in Beit Haifud. He did not send them home. He sold his silence to Mihail instead, and has been paid for it ever since out of Bloria's own money.

The factor's house

Vasselt keeps a narrow stone house near the cloth market, with a counting-room on the ground floor and no sign over the door. He is a man of about fifty, grey, short-sighted and courteous, who dresses like a cloth merchant and is taken for one by most of his neighbours. He came to Ocristan around 1479 AS, after the Battle of Crow's Pass had made Mihail worth backing, and has outlasted two kings in the city.

He keeps no guards that anyone has seen. His safety is the bills. Killing him would stop the money.

The bills

Bloria does not send Mihail coin. Coin can be followed on the road, taxed at the river and stolen. It sends paper: letters of credit drawn on the Ostvald houses, the great Blorian banking families that have lent to the crown for generations. Vasselt countersigns each bill, and it can be cashed only by the person named on it, at a house that will honour it.

The bills are sealed in red wax. The wax is mixed with blood drawn from debtors in Blorian prisons, one debtor to one seal, and the debtor's name is written on the back of the bill. A bill presented by anyone other than its payee is said to blister the hand that holds it, and a forged seal to rot (see Blood-Seals). When a bill is paid out, the debtor whose blood is in it is released, or so the Ostvald houses claim. Prisoners in Bloria are said to fear being bled for a large bill, because the larger the sum the longer it is held unpaid.

The paper leaves a trail, and Bloria keeps the other end of it. Every sum Mihail has spent of Bloria's money is written down in a Blorian ledger, under his name.

The terms

Mihail signed the Ustrus loan in the southern tongue in Beit Haifud in 1487 AS. See The Gold Run. A fair copy was kept in his chancery in Ocristan. Over the winter a clerk of that chancery made a second copy and sold it to Vasselt. The clerk was found in the town ditch before the thaw; see The Drowned Clerk. Whether Vasselt or Mihail had him drowned, or neither, is unknown.

What the terms say is not known to this atlas. Vasselt's conduct since suggests they would cost Mihail Bloria's money if Bloria read them. That fits the common reading, that the loan is to be repaid in war on Bloria (see Tamarun), but proves nothing.

Vasselt did not send the copy north. He told Mihail he had it. Since then about a tenth of every Blorian bill has come back to him, cashed by a payee of Mihail's choosing and passed on in coin. Bloria, in effect, pays its factor to keep Bloria uninformed.

What Bloria knows

  • The loan. Bloria knows Mihail took Ustrus gold; everyone in Ocristan knows. It does not know the terms, unless someone other than Vasselt has sent them.
  • The embassy. Vasselt knew Mihail had left Ocristan in the spring of 1488 AS. Whether he knew where Mihail went, or reported his absence, is unknown. See The Mechil Embassy.
  • The factor. Bloria has kept paying through Vasselt. Either it trusts him, or it has a second eye in Ocristan watching both of them. In the winter of 1487 AS the Ostvald houses sent a reckoner, Wendel Raust, to read his books.

Which of the Thirteen stands behind Bloria, and whether it reads the factor's letters, is unknown.

Loose threads

  • Who drowned the chancery clerk, and does anyone else hold a copy of the terms? Answered in part: by the wardens' finding, nobody; he drowned himself, and his note says he sold the terms twice. See The Drowned Clerk. The second buyer is unknown.
  • Whose blood is in Mihail's bills? A debtor's name is written on the back of each one, and somebody in Ocristan has kept the paper. Narrowed: debtors in the Ostvald Bonds, a few of them from Veleh villages. See The Ostvald Houses.
  • If Bloria has a second eye in Ocristan, who is it watching: Mihail, or Vasselt? Narrowed: an Ostvald reckoner, Wendel Raust, audited Vasselt's books over the winter of 1487 AS. What he found is unknown.
