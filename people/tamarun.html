Tamarun is the Emperor of Ustrus, the overlord-by-proxy of Veleh. He is young, looks bored and is sharper than he looks. In 1487 AS he went around his own empire's revenue office and lent Baron Mihail the gold that bought Veleh's council. His aim, as far as it can be read, was to turn Veleh against Bloria.

The court

Tamarun receives in a throne room of marble and sheet gold under painted ceilings, on a throne the size of a market stall. Ten or twelve guards stand around it, each a head taller than an ordinary man, in armour that gives off a faint blue light. Veleh soldiers who have seen them say plainly that fighting them would be suicide. Rumour in Veleh gives him a hundred wives from across the world. The rumour is older than he is and has been said of every emperor before him.

Those who have stood before him describe a man in his early twenties who looks as if the audience bores him, and who misses nothing.

The loan

In 1487 AS Mihail came to Beit Haifud having helped bring down Ustrus's own king in Veleh, Olegzander. Tamarun received him in the southern tongue, opened a chest of gold, and lent it to him. The terms were written and signed, and have not been published.

Olegzander had been the man of the Counting-House, not the Emperor's. He was a tax collector, good for taking half a village's grain and useless for anything else. Mihail was different: a warlord who had taken the Rift, with a bound company as his sword-arm and the barons bought or dead behind him. A tax farm cannot fight a war. Mihail's Veleh could.

The war Tamarun wants is with Bloria, the empire in the north-west, which has fought Ustrus by proxy for generations. Bloria is also Mihail's paymaster. It is widely suspected, though not confirmed, that the loan is to be repaid in war: Veleh, under Mihail, turned against the empire that pays him. If so, Tamarun lent gold to a Blorian proxy in order to point him at Bloria.

Two wills

The Counting-House did not agree. It hunted Mihail on the way south, doubled the southern garrisons after the audience and sent galleys after the gold. Its interest in Veleh is revenue, in goods and in people. A Veleh armed for war pays less tax and gives fewer bodies. The Emperor wants a sword and the House wants a farm. Both claim to speak for Ustrus.

What he knows

Whether Tamarun knows what stands behind his throne is unconfirmed. Signs point both ways.

  • For. He went around the Counting-House deliberately and quietly, as a man would who knows whose office it is. He has never been heard to speak of the House by name.
  • Against. He acts as though Ustrus's policy were his to set. A man who knew what the House answers to might not dare cross it, or might not survive having done so.

He has survived so far.

Loose threads

  • If the loan is to be repaid in war on Bloria, Bloria is funding a man Ustrus is arming against it. Who in Bloria has read the terms? Answered in part: Bloria's factor in Ocristan, Edrun Vasselt, has a copy and has kept it from Bloria.
  • The Counting-House failed to stop the gold. Has it moved against the Emperor since, or he against it? Narrowed: in the spring of 1488 AS his name was written, unmarked, in the margin of the house's second ledger. What that means is unknown. See The Margin.
  • Men of the escort say the Emperor watched Elric longer than Mihail through the whole audience.
