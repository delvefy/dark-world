The Blackmire is a fen in the wet low country south of the Rift, where the Krisen and the Olta run down to the Vorn Danu. As ground it is ordinary: peat, standing water and alder. It is known for one thing. A line of women born there sell years of life and take the price in other lives. Wherever they go, they are called the Blackmire witches.

The fen

The mire fills the low triangle between three rivers. In spring the Vorn Danu backs up into it and the water stands chest-deep for weeks. The peat is dark enough to stain the water like tea, which gives the place its name. Peat-cutters from the villages on its edge work the margins. They do not go further in than they can see.

No road crosses it. The road from the Rift to the Vorn Danu crossing goes around its eastern side. Tax riders went around it too, even under Olegzander, who had more reason than most to know what lived there.

Nothing about the fen is known to be unnatural. Bodies found in the peat are well kept, but bog bodies are well kept everywhere. Those who have looked for a source of the witches' power in the ground, a relic, a buried thing, a stone, have found nothing.

The line

The power is in the blood, not the place. The witches are women of one family, or of a few families that have married into each other for so long that the difference no longer matters. A daughter of the line is a Blackmire witch whether she grows up in the fen or a hundred miles from it. Most stay. Some travel to a buyer when the price is high enough. The witch who raised Mihail came north to Crow's Pass to do it.

How many there are is unknown. Estimates in Veleh run from a handful to several dozen. No witch has ever said.

The bargain

A Blackmire witch sells time: years added to a life, or a life brought back after it has ended. The price is always other lives, named by the witch, and often named after the work is done. The buyer's own flesh pays interest on whatever is still owed. Olegzander's rot is the best-recorded case.

Every debt is inherited, in both directions.

  • The buyer's side. If a buyer dies before the price is paid in full, the rest falls on his heirs. Where there are no heirs, it is said to fall on whoever lived under him: his household, then his tenants, then his villages.
  • The witch's side. If a witch dies, her daughter takes her ledger. The bargain does not end and the debt is not forgiven. It is commonly said that the daughter adds a penalty for the death.

This is why killing a witch is thought pointless. The debt survives her, and so does the family.

A necessary evil

Nobody in Veleh likes the Blackmire, and nobody moves against it. The reasons differ by rank.

  • Villagers and small lords are too weak. A burned witch leaves a daughter, and the daughter remembers.
  • Barons and kings could empty the fen with a few hundred men, but the gain is not worth the trouble. Debts would pass to the next witch, and the line has relatives nobody can count.
  • The empires leave it alone for the same reason, as far as anyone can tell. No mage corps is known to have hunted the line. Whether any of the Thirteen has dealt with it is unknown.

So the witches serve no side. Mihail's family paid them to raise him in 1472 AS. Olegzander, who would later be his rival for the crown, paid them to keep his rotting body upright. Both bargains were made with the same line, and neither buyer could stop the other from making his.

The children of Crow's Pass

The price for Mihail's return was his three youngest children. What became of them is unknown. Three stories are told in Veleh:

  • They were drowned in the fen, and the peat has kept them.
  • They are held alive somewhere in the mire, as security against the bargain.
  • They were taken into the line and raised as its own.

None has any evidence behind it. The witches do not answer questions about past bargains.

Loose threads

  • Olegzander died owing. His heirs are unknown. Is someone in his old villages already paying? Narrowed: not in Haldevin, where the children died of hunger, not of any witch's debt. Elsewhere, unknown.
  • Lord Andrej's mind-witch was killed in the forest skirmishes before the fall of the Rift. Was she of the line, and has a daughter come to collect? Answered: no; she was of Andrej's own household.
  • A debt on a dead man falls on his heirs. Mihail died and came back. Who is the heir of a revenant?
