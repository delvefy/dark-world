Kalderhof is a walled crown estate in the far north of Bloria, at the end of the roads the Ostvald night-wagons take. Every Blorian knows the name, and it is used to frighten children and debtors alike. No one outside the escort is known to have passed its gate. The common belief is that the bonded are not killed there but spent: kept alive while their years are taken from them, one at a time, for the power behind the Blorian throne.

The estate

Kalderhof lies on a high, treeless moor north of the range, several days beyond the last Blorian market town. It appears in crown rolls as a demesne farm, with a steward, a chapel and a tithe of nothing. The wall is real; drovers who have strayed onto the moor describe it as low, grey and very long, with a single gate on the south side and no towers. Smoke rises from inside it in every season. Nobody has reported the sound of livestock.

The roads to it are kept in better repair than any other roads in the north. The inns along them are crown property and close their shutters at dusk.

The wagons

A debtor of the Ostvald houses whose bill has gone seven years uncashed is moved to the lower bond and taken north by night, in a closed wagon under Ostvald escort. The wagons travel in small trains, two or three at a time, about once a month from each of the larger Bonds. They do not stop in towns. By the last stage the escort has changed twice, and the final drivers are men who do not go south again.

The wagons come back empty. Once or twice a year a different train comes south from the gate, carrying sealed casks. The casks hold a fine grey ash. It is spread on crown fields, and those fields are said to yield well.

What is done there

What happens inside the wall is unknown. The account most Blorians believe runs as follows, and none of it is confirmed:

  • The bonded are not killed on arrival. They are fed, housed and kept warm, better than they were in the Bonds.
  • Each year something is taken from them, and they grow older by more than a year. Prisoners who arrive young are said to be grey within five winters.
  • When there is nothing left to take, they die, and are burned. The ash is what comes south.
  • The years are not wasted. They go to whatever stands behind the Blorian crown, which is how it has lasted.

The witches of the Blackmire are known to sell years and take payment in other lives. Whether Kalderhof does by law and ledger what the Blackmire does by bargain, and whether the two methods share a source, is unknown. No one has been able to compare them.

Who it serves

On paper Kalderhof belongs to the crown, and the crown owes the Ostvald houses more than it can pay. If the estate serves the one of the Thirteen said to rule behind Bloria, the Lady of the Ledger, as the story holds, then the debts, the Bonds and the lower bond are one machine, and the money is only the part that shows. This is the common reading. It is unconfirmed.

Kalderhof is also the plainest explanation for an old Blorian peculiarity: the houses do not want their loans repaid. A debt paid frees a debtor. A debt held seven years sends one north.

In Veleh

Some of the names on the backs of the Ostvald bills that fund Baron Mihail belong to Veleh villages north of the range. If those bills go uncashed for seven years, those debtors go to Kalderhof. Mihail is known to draw slowly. Whether he knows what the delay costs is unknown.

Loose threads

  • The final drivers do not go south again. Where do they live, and does anyone in Bloria count them?
  • The ash fields are said to yield well. Who eats what grows on them?
  • If a debtor's bill is cashed after the debtor has gone north, is anyone ever sent back? Narrowed: nobody is known to have been. A debtor who dies there voids his bill; its seal goes to grey powder. See Blood-Seals.
