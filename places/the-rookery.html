The Rookery

Realm
Veleh, in the north-eastern forest, about a day south of the Mechil border
Kept by
A mute order of brothers. Their rule forbids speech for life.
Own name
Never spoken, so not known. Veleh calls it the Rookery.
Taxed by
No one, in any record
Known for
Its crows; its walking brothers; the Mechil Embassy of 1488 AS

The Rookery is a monastery of a mute order in the forests of north-eastern Veleh, close to the border with Mechil. Its brothers take a vow of silence for life. Many thousands of crows roost in the woods around it, and the brothers feed them every dawn. Veleh lords have never taxed it and Mechil has never touched it. In the spring of 1488 AS Baron Mihail met the envoys of Mechil in its guest hall. It is widely believed in the north-east that Mechil learns what happens in Veleh from crows, and that the Rookery is where it listens.

The house

The monastery stands on a low rise in old oak forest, a long day's ride north-east of Ocristan and less than a day from the border. It is built of dark timber on a stone footing: a church with a single onion-shaped dome, a ring of cells, a refectory, a guest hall and a bell-frame with no bell. The brothers do not ring bells. They mark the hours with a wooden board struck with a mallet, which carries a long way through the trees.

How old the house is is not recorded. Veleh charters older than the kingdom mention a house of silent brothers in the north-eastern forest, without a name. The brothers keep no written records that anyone outside has seen.

The silence

A novice is received without words. He is shaved, given a black habit and a wooden clapper, and from that day does not speak again. The brothers communicate by signs among themselves. They are not known to write to anyone outside the house.

The rule has one penalty that people in the north-east know of. A brother who speaks is taken north across the border by other brothers and left in Mechil. None is known to have come back. What Mechil does with them is unknown.

Not all the brothers are from Veleh. One old brother of the house is said to be Kaspar Hollmar, a Blorian banker who fled Aldrecht c. 1458 AS after saying one thing too many at court.

The walking brothers

Some of the brothers leave the house for years at a time and walk the roads of Veleh, begging. They carry the clapper and a wooden bowl, sleep in barns and church porches, and are given bread almost everywhere. A man who cannot speak cannot carry tales, and people talk freely in front of them. Walking brothers have been seen in every part of Veleh: in the mill country, at the Rift, in the swamps of Olta, on the Vorn Danu road. One was seen at Trantar in the spring of 1488 AS, a few days before the rising broke.

The brothers do not write and do not speak. Nobody has found a way for what they hear to reach anyone. That is the difficulty with the common belief about them, and the reason the common belief adds the crows.

The crows

The forest around the Rookery is the largest crow roost in Veleh. At dawn the brothers go out into the clearings with bread and stand among the birds while they feed. Travellers who have watched say the brothers stand very still for a long time, heads a little on one side, as if listening. Then they go back inside.

The belief in the north-east is simple. Crows go everywhere and are noticed by no one. Mechil reads them, and the Rookery is where they come home. Nothing proves it. It is unconfirmed, and most of Veleh outside the north-east regards it as a peasant story.

The story has been repeated more since 1488 AS. A flock of crows sat on the western tower of Trantar when the Hundred-Eleven rode in and did not move. The crows of the range roost in hundreds on the arch at Crow's Pass, where Mihail was brought back. And at the embassy, the envoys of Mechil knew that Trantar had fallen before Mihail did.

The embassy

In the spring of 1488 AS, while the company fought at Trantar, Baron Mihail rode north-east with a small escort and met envoys of Mechil in the Rookery's guest hall. The brothers served bread and water and left the room. The meeting lasted two days. Mihail offered to default on his Ustrus loan if Mechil would stand behind him. The envoys listened and gave no answer. See The Mechil Embassy.

Men of Mihail's escort describe the envoys as grey-coated, close-shaven and courteous. They did not smile in two days. On the first morning, they said, one envoy went out into the clearing at dawn with the brothers and stood among the crows. On the second day the envoys asked how many of the Hundred-Eleven were still alive.

Mechil and the house

Mechil has not moved on Veleh in about a hundred years. The last time it did, around 1388 AS, its army came south through the north-eastern forest and passed within sight of the Rookery. It burned two villages within a day's march and did not set foot in the monastery's clearing. Veleh lords have left the house alone as well. None of them has ever said why. Some of them have said that they would not like to find out.

Which of the Thirteen stands behind Mechil, and whether it has anything to do with the house, is unknown.

Loose threads

  • A brother who speaks is left in Mechil. What is done with a man whose only offence is having spoken?
  • Do the walking brothers cross into Bloria and Ustrus as well, and is there a rookery in either?
  • The army of c. 1388 AS passed the house by. Was it marching somewhere else, or had it come to see that the house still stood?
