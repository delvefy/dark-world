Crow's Pass

Realm
Veleh, on the Bloria border
Range
The north-western end of the northern range, above the Olta headwaters
Feature
The Crow Gate: a freestanding kimberstone arch, older than the Silence
Held by
A garrison of Baron Mihail's household, since 1472 AS
Known for
The battle of 1472 AS, and Mihail's return

Crow's Pass is a high saddle at the north-western end of the northern range, where an old drove road crosses from Veleh into Bloria. At its summit stands a single arch of kimberstone, with no wall on either side of it and no road that needs it. Baron Mihail died beside it in 1472 AS and was brought back on its sill. His household has held the pass ever since and has never said why.

The road

The pass climbs out of the Olta headwaters, north of the mill country around Haldevin, and comes down on the Blorian side two days later. It was a drovers' road and a smugglers' road, never a trade road. In winter it is closed by snow for months at a time. Marcher lords on both sides took tolls on it whenever they were strong enough to hold the summit.

Since 1472 AS the road has been little used. Drovers say the garrison does not stop them. They go the long way round anyway.

The Crow Gate

The arch stands across the road at the top of the saddle, about four times the height of a man and wide enough for a cart. Like all the builders' work it is white, seamless and unmarked by weather. Nothing is attached to it. No foundation has been found for a wall or a gatehouse, and the ground on either side falls away to scree. The road simply passes under it, as it might pass under a tree.

What it was for is unknown. An arch across a road that needs no gate is the kind of thing the builders left in several places, and nobody has explained any of them.

The crows of the range roost on it in hundreds. Kimberstone takes no stain, and the arch stays white under them. The pass takes its name from the birds; the name is older than the battle.

The battle and the sill

In 1472 AS Mihail's levies held the summit against the riders of a Blorian marcher lord, in a quarrel over the tolls. The fight was small. Mihail took three arrows in the chest near the arch and died there. See Battle of Crow's Pass.

His men did not carry him far. The witch of the Blackmire line whom his family sent for came north to the pass, not to his house, and did the work at the arch itself. Accounts from his household agree on one point: she had the body laid across the sill, the flat stone under the arch, with the head on the Blorian side and the feet in Veleh. They do not agree on anything after that.

The sill now carries a dark brown mark the length and shape of a lying man. Kimberstone takes no stain. Blood washes off it, rain runs off it, and the rest of the arch is as white as the day it was made. This mark is the only stain on kimberstone that anyone has recorded.

What went through

It is said in Veleh that something passed under the arch that night, one way or the other. Three readings are current:

  • Mihail came back through it. The arch is a door to wherever the dead go, and the witch used it to fetch him.
  • Something else came with him. What walked back under the arch is wearing Mihail.
  • Something went out. The price was paid at the arch, and what was paid went through it, the other way.

None is proven. Whether the arch had ever been used this way before, or since, is unknown.

The garrison

About twenty men of Mihail's household keep the pass, in a timber blockhouse below the summit on the Veleh side. They are, by most accounts, the same men who carried him off the field in 1472 AS, and they have not been relieved in sixteen years. They age like other men. They let travellers through. They do not let anyone stand under the arch, or touch the sill.

Mihail has never explained the garrison. He kept it through the Rift campaign, when he needed every man, and through the buying of Ocristan, when he could least afford it.

Bloria began paying Mihail some years after the battle. Its factor, Edrun Vasselt, came to Ocristan around 1479 AS. Whether what Blorian riders saw at the pass in 1472 AS had any part in that is unknown.

Loose threads

  • The garrison keeps people away from the sill. Is it keeping something in, or keeping the way open?
  • The Blorian dead of 1472 AS were buried in a pit below the arch, on the Blorian side. Nobody from Bloria has come for them.
  • Are there other arches like the Crow Gate, and has anyone else been laid across one?
